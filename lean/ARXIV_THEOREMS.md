## §§1–3. Main theorems and the construction

In §§1–8, $D$ is one specific subring of $\mathbb Q(t)$, $A=D\cap\mathbb Q[t]$ is its polynomial part, and $T$ is a unital subring. Units, divisibility and irreducibility in $T$ are intrinsic to $T$.

For the remark, both proofs start with the finitely many monic polynomial divisors $H$ of $f$. The draft fixes the guard profile and recovers the rational scalar $c$ up to sign. Lean clears denominators at all guards for $cH$ and its complementary factor $c^{-1}(f/H)$. This puts both $c$ and $c^{-1}$ over fixed denominators, leaving finitely many choices for $c$. The public example specializes the conclusion to $t^2$ and exhibits its factorization $t\cdot t$. **Applications of cited literature.** After Proposition 8.1, the draft applies Spirito’s result on the Jacobson radical and gives a direct argument that $J(D)=0$ from eventual guard units. In §8.2 the profile of a nonzero integral ideal is the norm of Coykendall–Hasenauer (Definition 2.8 and Theorems 2.9–2.10). For a nonzero fractional ideal $J$, clearing a denominator $d$ gives $v_M(J)=v_M(dJ)-v_M(d)$ at every maximal ideal $M$. After Proposition 8.3 the draft interprets the restricted profiles as compactly supported continuous functions on the maximal spectrum in the inverse topology. After Proposition 8.4 it applies Chang’s formula to obtain $\operatorname{Pic}(A)\cong\bigoplus_p\mathbb Z$. These applications and the direct Jacobson radical argument are outside the checked results in the table.
